§ The Genius

Srinivasa Ramanujan

Srinivasa Ramanujan
22 Dec 1887 · 26 Apr 1920
FRS · Mathematical Genius

Srinivasa Ramanujan was an Indian mathematician who lived during the British Rule in India. Though he had almost no formal training in pure mathematics, he made extraordinary contributions to mathematical analysis, number theory, infinite series, and continued fractions.

Born in Erode, Tamil Nadu on 22 December 1887, Ramanujan displayed an uncanny ability with numbers from childhood. By age 15 he had mastered advanced trigonometry and discovered theorems independently.

His collaboration with G.H. Hardy at Cambridge became legendary. Hardy called their partnership one of the most remarkable in mathematical history, marveling at Ramanujan's intuitive leaps — results that seemed to come from nowhere yet proved rigorously correct.

Among his enduring creations is the famous 4×4 Magic Square derived from his own date of birth — a beautiful demonstration of the hidden order concealed within numbers.

Anatomy of the Magic Square

Structure

Row 1 — The date of birth: Day · Month · Century · Year
Rows 2–4 — Derived values ensuring every row, column, diagonal sums to the magic number

Magic sum for Ramanujan's square = 22 + 12 + 18 + 87 = 139

The Pattern Formula

Let d = day, m = month, c = century digits, y = year digits:

Row 1: d · m · c · y
Row 2: [y + 1, c - 1, m - 3, d + 3]
Row 3: [m - 2, d + 2, y + 2, c - 2]
Row 4: [c + 1, y - 1, d + 1, m - 1]
